    The big picture

    How cost, debt, graduation and pay relate across colleges

    How to read this
    • Each dot is one college, colored by type of college.
    • Each small box compares two things. The name under the column says what goes across; the name at the start of the row says what goes up.
    • Dots rising from left to right mean the two things go up together. A shapeless cloud means they aren't related.
    • The grey boxes on the diagonal show how many colleges have each value. Taller bars mean more colleges.
    • Try it: drag a box to highlight those colleges everywhere, click a dot to pin it, or double-click a box to open it bigger in plot 2.

    Zoom in on one trade-off

    Pick two things to compare and find the best deals

    How to read this
    • Each dot is one college, placed by the two things you pick with the buttons below.
    • The "preferred corner" label shows where most students want to be, for example low cost and high pay.
    • Turn on "Show best deals" to draw a line through the colleges that no other college beats on both things at once.
    • It is not a ranking. A college below the line can still be the right choice once you think about other things.
    • Try it: click a dot to pin a college, or drag a box to select a group.

    Compare your shortlist

    Your pinned colleges side by side, against typical colleges

    How to read this
    • Each row is one question: cost, debt, graduation rate and pay, each on its own scale.
    • The numbered dots are your pinned colleges, with the same numbers as everywhere else.
    • The grey bar covers the middle half of colleges that match your filters, and the dashed line is the typical college.
    • A dot outside the grey bar is unusually high or low on that question. Each row says whether lower or higher is better.
    • Exact numbers are in the table underneath.

    Check your major

    Student debt and pay for one major, college by college

    How to read this
    • Pick a major in the "Major" filter at the top. Each dot is that major at one college.
    • Further left means students borrow less; higher up means graduates earn more.
    • The dashed lines show the typical debt and pay for this major.
    • For pinned colleges, the square is the college overall and the arrow points to this major. An arrow pointing up means this major pays more than the college's average.
    • "All majors at one college" shows every major at one of your pinned colleges instead.
